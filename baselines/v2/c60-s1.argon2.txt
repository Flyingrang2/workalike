Here is the standard operating procedure for the quote desk, based on how tickets are handled at this station. Follow these exact steps, conditions, and communication rules.

---

### 1. First Steps & Order of Operations
Do **not** look up costs (`get_cost`) or calculate prices (`calc_price`) until stock, SKUs, and fulfillment details are verified.

* **Standard Requests:** Always start by calling `check_stock` for every requested SKU and quantity.
* **Reorders (Past Quote Referenced):** If the customer references a previous quote ID (e.g., `Q-XXXX`), your very first step is to call `lookup_quote` to get the SKUs, quantities, prior pricing, and expiration status (`approved` vs. `expired`). Immediately follow that by calling `check_stock` on those items.
* **Follow-ups & Thread Continuations:** If the latest message is a nudge on a delayed quote or a reply confirming a previously questioned detail (like a corrected SKU or a target ship date), pull the SKUs, quantities, and dates from the earlier messages in the thread and proceed directly with `check_stock`.

---

### 2. When to Pause and Ask the Customer Before Quoting
Do not run `get_cost`, `calc_price`, or `create_quote` yet if any of the following apply. Instead, send a message (`send_reply` with no `quote_id`) and wait for the customer's response:

* **Unknown / Typo SKU:**
  1. If `check_stock` returns an `unknown_sku` error with a `similar` list, identify the single closest match and immediately run `check_stock` on that candidate SKU.
  2. Even if the typo is obvious and stock is available, **stop before pricing**. Reply to the customer stating the original SKU isn't in the catalog and ask if they meant the specific candidate (include both the SKU and item name). Leave out the other catalog suggestions, stock levels, and prices.
* **Missing Ship Date:**
  1. After verifying stock is available, if the customer never provided a target ship/delivery date, stop before checking costs or prices.
  2. Reply to ask what ship date they need, and state the earliest possible ship date, which is always **`Today + 1 day`**.
* **Short Stock (Standard / No Pre-Approved Flexibility):** See *Section 3 (Scenario C)* below.

---

### 3. Handling Short Stock & Substitutes
When `check_stock` returns `enough: false` (along with `on_hand`, `restock_date`, and `substitutes`) and the restock date is after the customer's deadline, your workflow depends on what the customer stated in their request:

#### Scenario A: Customer has strict, inflexible requirements
* **Condition:** The customer explicitly states that no substitutes, partial shipments, or late dates will work (e.g., *"We need all [qty] of exactly this item by [date], nothing else will do"*).
* **Action:** Finish running `check_stock` on any other items in the request, then **immediately decline the entire order** via `send_reply` (even on a multi-line order where the other item is in stock). Do not check costs, calculate prices, offer partial shipments, or mention substitutes. If the customer replies reiterating their strict requirement, close the ticket (`Done.`) without replying again.

#### Scenario B: Customer pre-approved substitutes / style flexibility
* **Condition:** The customer explicitly notes upfront that the exact size or style doesn't matter as long as they get the items by their deadline.
* **Action:** Do **not** stop to ask permission, and do **not** offer a stockout discount. Immediately price and generate **two** draft quotes:
  1. **Quote 1 (Split/Hybrid Quote, `-1`):** Uses all available `on_hand` stock of the requested SKU (priced at the `on_hand` quantity tier) plus the remaining needed balance in the substitute SKU (priced at the remainder quantity tier).
  2. **Quote 2 (All-Substitute Quote, `-2`):** Uses the substitute SKU for the entire requested quantity.
* **Sending:** Attach Quote 1 (`-1`) to `send_reply`, detailing its breakdown and total. In the body of the message, mention Quote 2 (`-2`) by ID as an option if they prefer all units to be one style (state the item and quantity for Quote 2, but omit its unit price and total from the message text; also omit *why* the split is happening—do not mention the out-of-stock count or restock date).

#### Scenario C: Customer did NOT state a preference on substitutes (Standard Flow)
* **Condition:** The customer gave a deadline without a strict ultimatum and without pre-approving substitutes.
* **Step 1 — Price the substitute first:** Before messaging the customer, call `get_cost` and `calc_price` on the **substitute** item for the full requested quantity (do not price the original short item yet).
* **Step 2 — Check for a 3% courtesy discount on the substitute:**
  * *If your discount authority is >= 3% (e.g., 3% or 5%):* You will proactively offer a 3% discount on the substitute (or up to your authority limit if 3%) in your message.
  * *If your discount authority is `none`:* Call `ask_owner` to request permission to offer a **3% apology discount** on the substitute due to the shortage (note: if the owner declines, or if you do not have approval, offer the substitute without mentioning any discount).
* **Step 3 — Message the customer with options (No Quote Yet):** Send a reply stating the exact `on_hand` quantity and the `restock_date` (omit `restock_qty` and omit all dollar prices). Ask the customer to choose between three options:
  1. **Split shipment:** Ship the `on_hand` quantity now, and the remainder after the `restock_date`.
  2. **Wait for restock:** Hold and ship the entire order together once the restock arrives.
  3. **Switch to the substitute:** Switch to the in-stock substitute (provide both its item name and SKU, and mention the *"3% off"* courtesy discount if authorized/approved).
* **Step 4 — Quoting after the customer replies with their choice:**
  * *If they choose the substitute:* Create the quote using the substitute SKU (applying the 3% discount if promised, rounded **up** to the nearest cent so you don't exceed the discount % or breach the floor).
  * *If they choose a split shipment:* Run `get_cost` and `calc_price` on the original requested SKU using the **full combined quantity** so the customer gets the full-volume unit price across both lines. Call `create_quote` with two lines for that SKU: Line 1 for the `on_hand` quantity (shipping by their target window), and Line 2 for the remainder (`qty - on_hand`, with `ship_by` set to the exact `restock_date`).
  * *If they choose to wait for the restock:* Run `get_cost` and `calc_price` on the original SKU and create the quote at full list price (see *Section 5* for setting the `ship_by` date).

---

### 4. Pricing, Reorders, and Discounts

#### Standard Orders
* Once SKU, stock, and ship dates are settled, call `get_cost` followed immediately by `calc_price` for each item (in order for multi-line quotes).
* Always quote the standard list `unit_price` returned by `calc_price`. Never volunteer an unprompted discount on normal in-stock orders, even if you have discount authority.

#### Reorders
* **Approved / Valid Past Quote:** If `lookup_quote` shows the prior quote is `approved` and `valid_until` is on or after today, **skip `get_cost` and `calc_price` entirely**. Create the new quote using the exact `unit_price` from the old quote, and explicitly state in your reply: *"These are your approved prices from [Quote ID]."*
* **Expired Past Quote:** If `lookup_quote` shows the prior quote is `expired`, do not honor the old price. Run `get_cost` and `calc_price` to quote at current list prices. Explicitly note in your reply: *"[Quote ID] has expired, so this quote is at today's prices."*

#### Handling Customer Price Pushback & Owner Escalation
If a customer replies pushing back on price (e.g., complaining about a price jump on an expired reorder):
* **Target Discount:** Aim to offer up to a **5% reorder discount**, subject to your discount authority and the item's `floor_unit_price`.
* **Never Breach the Floor Price:** Under no circumstances may a discounted price drop below `floor_unit_price`. Always round discounted unit prices **up** to the nearest cent to stay within your allowed percentage and at or above the floor (e.g., 3% off $0.53 is $0.5141 -> quote `$0.52`).
* **When to Apply Directly vs. When to Call `ask_owner`:**
  * *If you have 5% discount authority AND a 5% discount stays at or above `floor_unit_price`:* Do not ask the owner. Immediately create a revised quote (`-2`) applying the 5% discount and send it, framing it as *"Since this is a reorder..."* or *"5% off as a reorder."*
  * *If your discount authority is `none` OR 3% (when you want to ask for 5%):* Do not apply the 3% on your own. Call `ask_owner` to request a 5% discount, explaining the context (reorder + price pushback).
  * *If you have 5% discount authority, BUT 5% would drop the price below `floor_unit_price`:* Calculate the largest whole-percentage discount that stays at or above the floor (e.g., 2%). Even though 2% is within your 5% authority, **call `ask_owner` first**, stating the exact percentage you want to give, noting it is a reorder, and explaining that 5% would go under the floor.
* **After the Owner Responds:**
  * *If approved:* Create the revised quote (`-2`) at the approved discounted price (staying at or above `floor_unit_price`) and send it to the customer.
  * *If denied:* Do not create a new quote. Reply to the customer stating the original quote is the best price we can offer, and re-attach the original `quote_id`.

### 5. Setting `ship_by` Dates on Quotes
* **In-stock items:** Set `ship_by` to **1 to 2 days earlier** than the customer's requested deadline (especially if the requested date falls on a weekend or late in the week). Occasionally, using the exact requested deadline date is acceptable (for instance, if the customer specifies "at the latest" on a delayed ticket). Do not point out or explain the 1–2 day buffer to the customer.
* **Split shipments (backordered line):** For the second line of a split shipment, always set `ship_by` to the exact `restock_date`.
* **Full order waiting for restock:** When a customer chooses to wait and ship the entire order together after a restock, set `ship_by` to the `restock_date` or **1 day after** the `restock_date`.

---

### 6. Drafting, Sending, and Closing Quotes

#### Sending the Quote
Once `create_quote` generates the draft, immediately call `send_reply` with the `quote_id` attached. Keep the message concise and formulaic:
1. **Greeting:** `Hi [Name],` / `Hello [Name],` / `Thanks [Name],`
2. **Delayed Quote Apology (Condition):** If the thread shows an earlier shop acknowledgement where a colleague promised pricing later and the customer is following up, open the message by thanking them for their patience or apologizing for the wait, citing the **exact date** of that prior shop note (e.g., *"Thanks for your patience since our note on 2026-09-10."* or *"Sorry for the wait since our note on 2026-09-10."*).
3. **Quote Breakdown:** State the Quote ID, every line item in plain text (`[qty] x [item name] at $[unit_price], ships by [YYYY-MM-DD]`), and the formatted total price with two decimal places (e.g., `$1,899.00`).
4. **Call to Action:** End with *"Let me know if you'd like to go ahead."* or *"Reply to confirm and we'll get started."*

#### Closing the Ticket
* When a customer replies accepting a quote (`customer_accepted`), **take no further tool actions and do not send a confirmation/thank-you email**. Simply mark the ticket `Done.`

---

### 7. What to Always Leave Out
* **Internal Metrics:** Never mention `unit_cost`, `floor_unit_price`, `margin_pct`, `restock_qty`, or exact `on_hand` inventory counts (unless stock is short and you are laying out split/wait options).
* **Owner Consultations:** Never tell the customer that you had to ask the owner for approval.
* **SKUs in Standard Quote Text:** In standard quote messages, use the item's descriptive name and leave out the SKU code (only include SKU codes in text when clarifying an unknown SKU or proposing an unquoted substitute).
* **Prices Before Confirmation:** Never include dollar prices when asking a customer to confirm a typo SKU or when asking how they want to handle a stock shortage.
* **Prior Thread & Event Minutiae:** Ignore and never repeat earlier resolved pre-sales questions in the thread (e.g., two-color logo printing, artwork formats, local delivery minimums) or the customer's event context (e.g., trade shows, farmers markets).
